# Foundation First — Next Steps / Answers to Your Questions

A useful estate plan begins by protecting people before optimizing everything else.

## Phase One — Put the foundation in place
The first phase is designed to move quickly. The goal is to create the core documents and decisions that protect your children, your assets, your wishes, and the people who may need authority to act if something happens to you.

That may include wills or trusts as appropriate, guardianship planning, powers of attorney, health-care directives, beneficiary coordination, and practical instructions that make the plan usable.

You do not need to solve every tax, investment, or wealth-transfer question before completing this foundation.

## Phase Two — Review and refine
Once the essential protections are in place, the analysis can widen. Phase Two can address tax planning, wealth-transfer strategies, asset protection, business interests, and other planning opportunities that deserve more specialized attention.

The sequence matters. A family should not remain exposed while advanced planning questions are being studied.

## Children first
For parents, one of the central purposes of an estate plan is to remove ambiguity. The plan should identify the people you trust, create authority where it is needed, coordinate the resources intended for your children, and reduce the chance that a court or institution must fill gaps you could have addressed yourself.

## Start with a conversation
You do not need a finished asset inventory to call. Begin with the people you want to protect and the decisions you do not want left to chance.
